## 1. Executive Summary & Vision

### Product Vision

Brandora AI is the operating system for social impact communication — a purpose-built, AI-native platform that transforms how sanitation, menstrual hygiene, and public health organizations build their digital presence, tell their stories, and earn institutional trust at scale.

We believe that the world's most important organizations — the ones fighting period poverty, building clean toilets in rural schools, distributing hygiene kits in slums — are chronically under-resourced when it comes to communications. They do world-changing work but look invisible online. Brandora AI closes that gap permanently.

### Mission Statement

To give every sanitation and menstrual hygiene organization — regardless of size or budget — the communication firepower of a Fortune 500 PR team, powered by AI that deeply understands their context, language, impact, and audience.

### North Star Metric

**"Trusted Posts Published Per Week"** — the number of posts generated, reviewed, and published by active organizations on Brandora AI. This metric captures activation, retention, and value delivered simultaneously. Target: 10,000 trusted posts/week by end of Year 1.

### Product Tagline

*"Built for impact. Designed for trust. Powered by AI."*

### Core Thesis

Existing social media and content tools are designed for marketers selling products. They optimize for virality, not credibility. They understand B2C consumer brands, not NGO program cycles, CSR compliance, SDG frameworks, or the cultural sensitivity required when discussing menstruation publicly in conservative communities. Brandora AI is the first tool built from first principles for this underserved, high-impact niche.

## 2. Problem Statement

### 2.1 The Core Problem

India has over 3.4 million registered NGOs — more per capita than almost any country on earth. Of those, approximately 85,000 work in WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene), and a growing number focus specifically on menstrual hygiene management (MHM). Globally, the number of organizations in this space — including CSR wings of corporations, UN-affiliated bodies, social enterprises, and grassroots collectives — runs into the hundreds of thousands.

Yet virtually all of them share the same painful communication reality:

**They do extraordinary work but appear ordinary — or invisible — online.**

### 2.2 Deep Problem Decomposition

#### Problem Layer 1: The Resource Gap

A typical mid-size NGO in India working on menstrual hygiene has:
- 1 communications staff member (often a junior program officer who "also handles social media")
- Zero dedicated content budget
- No access to professional copywriters who understand their sector
- No institutional knowledge about what content performs in this niche
- No time — their primary job is running programs, not generating LinkedIn posts

The result: sporadic posting, recycled stock photos, copy-pasted press releases, and a social presence that actively undermines donor trust rather than building it.

#### Problem Layer 2: The Cultural Sensitivity Gap

Menstrual hygiene is not a topic you can discuss the same way as, say, corporate sustainability. The language, framing, and cultural context matter enormously:

- What works in urban Mumbai doesn't work in rural Bihar
- What works in English doesn't translate directly to Hindi or Tamil
- Donors and CSR partners require evidence-based, impact-led framing
- Communities being served require dignity-centered, destigmatizing language
- Government and institutional audiences require policy-aligned, data-heavy framing

No general-purpose AI writing tool has been trained on this nuance. Generic tools produce generic output — often tone-deaf, sometimes actively harmful.

#### Problem Layer 3: The Trust Deficit

In the social impact sector, institutional credibility is existential. A single poorly worded post can:
- Alienate a major donor mid-campaign
- Trigger a CSR compliance audit
- Generate negative press coverage
- Destroy years of community trust

Organizations are therefore caught in a paralysis loop: they know they need to post more, but they're terrified of getting it wrong. This fear of misstepping leads to either silence or purely safe, entirely boring content that generates zero engagement.

#### Problem Layer 4: The Platform Complexity Gap

Modern digital presence requires simultaneous management of:
- LinkedIn (institutional donors, CSR managers, government officials)
- Instagram (younger donors, awareness campaigns, visual storytelling)
- Twitter/X (real-time campaigning, journalist engagement, policy advocacy)
- Facebook (community outreach, older donor base, regional groups)
- WhatsApp Business (grassroots community communication)

Each platform has different optimal formats, posting frequencies, hashtag strategies, and audience expectations. Managing all of them effectively requires a team. Most organizations have one person — if they're lucky.

#### Problem Layer 5: The Impact Storytelling Gap

The most powerful asset these organizations have — their actual field impact — is the least effectively communicated. A project that installed 500 toilets in 12 schools affecting 8,000 students becomes: "We completed our WASH project. Thank you to our donors."

The data is rich. The stories are there. The voices of beneficiaries are available. But organizations lack the frameworks, templates, and tools to transform raw impact data into compelling, shareable narratives that drive donations, CSR partnerships, and policy change.

#### Problem Layer 6: The Campaign Coordination Gap

Key awareness moments — World Menstrual Hygiene Day (May 28), World Toilet Day (November 19), Menstrual Hygiene Week, Swachh Bharat Abhiyan anniversaries — represent enormous opportunities to amplify reach and join global conversations. But most organizations:
- Don't plan campaigns in advance
- Miss the 2-week lead-up window that maximizes reach
- Post a single generic post on the day itself and wonder why it underperforms
- Have no content bank prepared for sustained campaign periods

### 2.3 The Consequence

The cumulative effect of these problems is a trust-funding death spiral:
- Poor digital presence → less visibility → fewer donors → less funding → smaller programs → less impact → weaker stories → even worse digital presence

Brandora AI breaks this cycle.

## 5. Market Gaps & Opportunities

### Gap 1: No Domain-Expert AI for Social Impact

Every AI writing tool available today is trained to produce marketing copy for commercial businesses. None has been trained on NGO impact reports, CSR compliance language, WASH program communication, menstrual health education frameworks, or SDG reporting. This creates a massive opportunity for a tool with embedded domain expertise.

**Opportunity:** Train and fine-tune AI models specifically on:
- 10,000+ NGO annual reports and impact communications
- UNICEF/WHO/USAID communication guidelines for WASH
- CSR compliance documentation formats
- MHM awareness campaign materials from successful global campaigns
- SDG communication frameworks

### Gap 2: No Cultural Sensitivity Layer for Menstrual Health Content

Writing about menstruation publicly in India requires navigating:
- Deep stigma in conservative communities
- Religious and caste dimensions
- Different vocabulary (menstruation, period, menses, "that time of month" — each has a different register and appropriate context)
- Images and visual language norms
- Platform-specific community standards

No tool today has a "menstrual health sensitivity filter" that checks content against these parameters before publishing.

**Opportunity:** Build a proprietary content safety layer specifically calibrated for menstrual health and sanitation communication that becomes an industry standard.

### Gap 3: No Impact-Data-to-Story Pipeline

Organizations collect field data (beneficiaries reached, toilets built, pads distributed, girls kept in school) but have no way to automatically transform this raw data into compelling social media narratives.

**Opportunity:** Build an "Impact → Story" pipeline where an organization inputs their program data (CSV or manual entry), and Brandora AI generates a full suite of content — LinkedIn post, Instagram carousel, Twitter thread, press release summary — all from the same data, with appropriate framing for each platform's audience.

### Gap 4: No CSR Calendar Intelligence

India's CSR ecosystem runs on calendars — fiscal year cycles, compliance deadlines, awareness days, government scheme anniversaries. No tool helps organizations plan their communications around these predictable moments.

**Opportunity:** Build a CSR-aware content calendar that automatically surfaces upcoming relevant dates (Swachh Bharat Abhiyan anniversary, Jal Jeevan Mission milestones, World Toilet Day, etc.) and generates campaign content proactively.

### Gap 5: No Multi-Stakeholder Content Differentiation

An organization communicating about a menstrual hygiene program needs to speak very differently to:
- Rural community beneficiaries (WhatsApp, local language, simple visuals)
- Urban donor prospects (LinkedIn, English, data-heavy)
- CSR committees (formal reports, compliance language, ROI focus)
- Journalists (press angle, statistics, human interest)
- Government officials (scheme alignment, policy language)

No current tool can generate audience-differentiated content for the same program automatically.

**Opportunity:** Build an "Audience Lens" system — one program, five communication outputs, each calibrated for a different stakeholder.

### Gap 6: No Hindi-First Social Impact Content Tool

The majority of India's NGO sector operates in Hindi or regional languages. All major AI content tools are English-first, with Hindi as an afterthought. This means:
- Hindi outputs are often grammatically awkward
- Cultural idioms are missed
- Nuances of rural vs. urban Hindi communication styles are lost

**Opportunity:** Build Hindi-first content generation with genuine cultural fluency, not just translation.

## 6. Unfair Advantage & Differentiators

### 6.1 Domain Model Advantage

Brandora AI will build a proprietary fine-tuned model trained on a curated corpus of:
- 50,000+ successful social media posts from leading sanitation/MHM organizations globally
- 10,000+ CSR reports with strong communications outcomes
- UNICEF, WHO, USAID communication guidelines
- MENSTRUAL hygiene communication best practices from MHM Hub, WASH United, Menstrual Health Alliance India

This domain knowledge becomes a **non-replicable moat**. A general tool cannot close this gap by adding a few prompts — it requires data collection, curation, fine-tuning, and continuous validation that only a focused vertical player can do.

### 6.2 Community Trust Flywheel

As more organizations use Brandora AI, we collect (with consent) anonymized performance data on what content works in this niche. This data trains better models, which creates better content, which attracts more organizations, which generates more data. This flywheel is unavailable to horizontal tools.

### 6.3 Cultural Intelligence Layer

Brandora AI employs a team of cultural consultants and domain experts from the WASH/MHM sector who validate and continuously improve the cultural sensitivity system. This human-in-the-loop quality control is not something a general AI tool can replicate without sector expertise.

### 6.4 Ecosystem Integration

We will integrate with the tools this sector actually uses:
- Salesforce Nonprofit Success Pack (NPSP) — for beneficiary data import
- CommCare/ODK — field data collection platforms
- eSamudaay / NGO-Connect — India NGO management platforms
- FCRA compliance reporting tools
- GiveIndia / Razorpay for Nonprofits — donation platform data

These integrations create switching costs and ecosystem lock-in that horizontal tools will never invest in building.

### 6.5 Regulatory Alignment

In India, CSR-funded organizations must comply with Ministry of Corporate Affairs reporting requirements. Brandora AI will generate content that is automatically aligned with these requirements — a feature that has zero competitors and directly reduces compliance risk for CSR managers.

### 6.6 Network Effects

Brandora AI's template library, hashtag intelligence, and campaign calendar all improve as more organizations in the same niche use the platform. A sanitation NGO in Maharashtra benefits from what's working for a peer organization in Tamil Nadu. This niche-specific network effect is impossible to replicate with a general tool.

## 13. AI Workflow Architecture

### 13.1 Multi-Model Routing Strategy

Brandora AI uses a **cost-intelligent, capability-matched** routing system that selects the right AI model for each task:

```
Content Request
     │
     ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│              Task Classification Layer               │
│         (Classify by: complexity, context,           │
│          language, platform, sensitivity)            │
└──────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                       │
          ┌────────────┼────────────────┐
          ▼            ▼                ▼
   [Simple/Fast]  [Complex/Rich]   [Sensitive/Hindi]
   GPT-4o-mini    Claude Sonnet    Claude Haiku
   or Gemini      or GPT-4o        (cultural routing)
   Flash 1.5
```

**Model Assignment Matrix:**

| Task | Primary Model | Fallback | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|

| Full campaign generation | Claude Sonnet | GPT-4o | Long context, nuanced tone |

### 13.2 Prompt Chain Architecture

```python
# Example: Impact Story Generation Pipeline

class ImpactStoryPipeline:

    async def run(self, impact_data: ImpactData, org_profile: OrgProfile) -> ContentBundle:

        # Step 1: Data Enrichment (Gemini Flash — fast, cheap)
        enriched_data = await self.enrich_data(impact_data)
        # → Adds SDG tags, generates impact equivalences ("enough to fill 2 Olympic pools")

        # Step 2: Narrative Core Generation (Claude Sonnet — narrative quality)
        story_core = await self.generate_narrative_core(enriched_data, org_profile)
        # → Generates: headline, hero sentence, 3 key proof points, CTA variants

        # Step 3: Platform Adaptation (GPT-4o-mini — fast parallel calls)
        platform_posts = await asyncio.gather(
            self.adapt_for_linkedin(story_core, org_profile),
            self.adapt_for_instagram(story_core, org_profile),
            self.adapt_for_twitter(story_core, org_profile),
            self.adapt_for_facebook(story_core, org_profile),
            self.adapt_for_whatsapp(story_core, org_profile),
        )

        # Step 4: Safety Check (Claude Haiku — binary classification)
        safety_results = await asyncio.gather(*[
            self.check_safety(post) for post in platform_posts
        ])

        # Step 5: Hashtag Injection (Hashtag Intelligence System)
        final_posts = await self.inject_hashtags(platform_posts, org_profile)

        return ContentBundle(posts=final_posts, safety=safety_results)
```

### 13.3 Cost Management Architecture

Tokens are expensive at scale. Cost optimization strategies:

**Caching Layer:**
- Cache identical or near-identical prompts (Redis, 24h TTL)
- Cache brand voice profiles (DB, updated on profile change)
- Cache hashtag clusters (Redis, refreshed weekly)

**Prompt Compression:**
- System prompts stored as compressed templates, expanded at runtime
- Historical context window limited to last 5 interactions
- Image descriptions pre-computed and cached

**Model Downgrade Logic:**
- If Claude Sonnet cost budget exceeded → route to GPT-4o-mini
- Daily AI cost cap per free tier user: $0.05
- Daily AI cost cap per paid user: $0.50 (monitored, alerted at 80%)

| Action | Token Budget | Target Cost |

```python
6. CTA (Final line): Clear, specific call to action

FORMAT RULES:
- Use short paragraphs (2-3 lines max)
- Use line breaks for visual breathing room
- NO generic corporate speak
- NO passive voice
- Avoid: "We are pleased to announce", "It is with great pleasure"
- DO use: "Here's what I/we learned:", "Real talk:", "This changed everything:"

SENSITIVITY RULES:
- If content involves menstruation: use destigmatizing, clinical but warm language
- Avoid euphemisms that reinforce shame: "that time of month", "lady problems"
- Preferred terms: "menstruation", "periods", "menstrual health"
- If discussing rural/marginalized communities: lead with dignity, not pity
- Beneficiaries are agents, not objects of charity
"""
```

## 22. Scheduling & Publishing System

### 22.1 Publishing Queue Architecture

```python
class PublishJob:
    id: UUID
    post_id: UUID
    platform: SocialPlatform
    scheduled_at: datetime (UTC)
    status: JobStatus           # queued / processing / published / failed / retrying
    retry_count: int            # max 3 retries
    error_message: str | None
    published_at: datetime | None
    platform_post_id: str | None  # ID returned by platform API
```

Celery beat scheduler processes the queue every 60 seconds:
- Picks up all jobs where `scheduled_at <= now + 5 minutes`
- Dispatches to platform-specific worker
- Worker calls platform API
- On success: update status, store `platform_post_id`
- On failure: retry with exponential backoff (1min, 5min, 15min)
- After 3 failures: alert user, mark as failed

## 25. AI Safety & Moderation

### 25.1 Content Safety Framework

Brandora AI handles uniquely sensitive content. Our safety system is not a generic content moderation layer — it is purpose-built for the menstrual health and sanitation space.

**Safety Classification Categories:**

| Category | Type | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Stigmatizing Language | Uses shame-reinforcing terms for menstruation | Auto-flag + suggest alternatives |
| Exploitative Imagery Prompts | Describes content that exploits beneficiary poverty | Block + explain |
| Medical Misinformation | Incorrect health claims about periods/sanitation | Flag + provide correct information |
| Culturally Inappropriate | Content that would cause offense in specific regional contexts | Amber flag + cultural note |
| Privacy Violation Risk | Content identifying vulnerable individuals without consent | Block + explain |
| Donor Compliance Risk | Content that could violate FCRA or CSR compliance | Amber flag + compliance note |
| Generic/Low Quality | Content that doesn't meet minimum quality bar | Soft flag + regeneration offer |

### 25.2 Sensitive Topic Handling

**Menstruation-Specific Rules:**
- Never use: "period problems", "feminine hygiene" (euphemistic), "that time of month"
- Preferred: "menstruation", "periods", "menstrual health", "menstrual hygiene"
- Visual references: Always dignified, never exploitative or clinical-cold
- Male audience sensitivity: frame as health/rights issue, not "women's issue only"

**Beneficiary Dignity Rules:**
- Never describe beneficiaries as "victims", "poor people", "unfortunates"
- Always: "community members", "program participants", "women in [location]"
- Consent model: testimonials require explicit consent flag in content creation form
- Child safety: never use identifiable images of minors in public-facing content

**Community Sensitivity Rules:**
- Rural community portrayals: avoid patronizing "we helped them" framing
- Caste sensitivity: avoid assumptions about communities that reinforce caste stereotypes
- Religious sensitivity: Navratri/menstruation intersections require specific handling

### 25.3 Human Review Triggers

Some content automatically requires human review before publishing:
- Contains identifiable beneficiary information
- References specific political figures or government officials by name
- Makes claims about specific competitor organizations
- Content score < 50
- User has overridden a safety flag

## 29. Prompt Engineering System

### 29.1 Prompt Architecture

All prompts in Brandora AI are structured as:

```python
class BrandoraPrompt:
    system_prompt: str      # Role + rules + cultural context (cached, ~500 tokens)
    context_block: str      # Org profile + voice profile (~200 tokens)
    task_prompt: str        # Specific generation task (~100-300 tokens)
    examples: List[str]     # 2-3 few-shot examples (~300 tokens)
    output_format: str      # JSON schema for structured output (~100 tokens)
```

**Total token budget per generation: ~1,200-1,500 tokens (input)**

### 29.2 System Prompt Components

```
ROLE: You are [domain expert role specific to task]

CONTEXT:
- Platform: [LinkedIn / Instagram / Twitter / Facebook]
- Organization: [name, type, focus area, geographic scope]
- Audience: [target audience for this specific post]
- Brand voice: [voice profile summary]
- Cultural context: [urban/rural India / global; sensitivity level]

RULES:
[Domain-specific rules for this content type]
[Cultural sensitivity rules]
[Platform technical rules — character limits, format]
[Quality rules — what makes great content in this niche]

AVOID:
[Explicit list of things to never do/say]

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Return a JSON object with:
{
  "post_content": "...",
  "hashtags": ["...", "..."],
  "quality_score": 0-100,
  "safety_flags": [],
  "posting_time_suggestion": "...",
  "alternative_hook": "..."
}
```

### 29.3 Chain-of-Thought for Complex Tasks

For impact story generation (complex multi-step reasoning):

```python
COT_IMPACT_STORY = """
Think through this step by step:

1. ANALYZE THE DATA:
   - What is the most surprising or counterintuitive statistic?
   - What is the human story behind the numbers?
   - What SDGs does this connect to?

2. IDENTIFY THE HOOK:
   - What would make someone stop scrolling?
   - What does the audience NOT know that they should?

3. BUILD THE NARRATIVE ARC:
   - Problem → Action → Result → Future

4. PLATFORM ADAPTATION:
   - For LinkedIn: lead with insight, close with invitation to discuss
   - For Instagram: lead with emotion, close with community action
   - For Twitter: lead with the most striking statistic

Now generate the content following this analysis.
"""
```

### 29.4 Prompt Versioning

All prompts are versioned in the database:
```sql
CREATE TABLE prompt_versions (
    id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
```

### 30.3 AI Cost Management at Scale

```python
class AICostManager:
    daily_budget_per_user = {
        "free": 0.05,   # $0.05/day
        "starter": 0.25,
        "growth": 1.00,
        "pro": 3.00,
        "enterprise": 10.00,  # per seat
    }

    def route_request(self, user, task_type, complexity):
        remaining_budget = self.get_remaining_daily_budget(user)
        
        if remaining_budget < 0.10 and user.plan == "free":
            return "gpt-4o-mini"  # cheapest capable model
        
        if complexity == "high" and remaining_budget > 0.20:
            return "claude-sonnet-3-7"
        
        return "gpt-4o"  # default capable model
```

**Cost reduction levers:**
1. Prompt caching (Anthropic prompt caching: 90% cost reduction on system prompts)
2. Response caching (Redis: identical requests return cached response)
3. Model downgrade (degraded quality, user notified, option to upgrade plan)
4. Batch processing (non-urgent AI tasks batched for off-peak pricing)

### 31.1 Authentication & Authorization

```python
# JWT Configuration
ACCESS_TOKEN_EXPIRE_MINUTES = 15

ALGORITHM = "RS256"  # Asymmetric keys

PUBLIC_ENDPOINTS = ["/health", "/auth/login", "/auth/register", "/auth/google"]

# Users can only access data belonging to their organization
```

### 31.2 Row-Level Security (Supabase)

```sql
-- Example RLS policy
CREATE POLICY "org_isolation" ON posts
  USING (org_id = auth.jwt() ->> 'org_id');

CREATE POLICY "user_roles" ON posts
  FOR INSERT USING (
    EXISTS (
      SELECT 1 FROM org_members
      WHERE org_id = posts.org_id
      AND user_id = auth.uid()
      AND role IN ('owner', 'admin', 'editor')
    )
  );
```

### 31.3 Social Account Security

Social platform OAuth tokens are stored with:
- Encryption: AES-256 encrypted at rest in database
- Access: Only decrypted at the moment of API call; never exposed to frontend
- Revocation: Users can revoke access at any time; tokens deleted from DB
- Scope: Minimum necessary OAuth scopes requested
- Audit: All social API calls logged for security review

### 31.4 Data Privacy

**GDPR Compliance:**
- Data Processing Agreement (DPA) available for EU organizations
- Right to deletion: API endpoint to delete all user data within 30 days
- Data portability: Export all content as JSON
- Cookie consent: Minimal cookies; analytics opt-out

**India IT Act Compliance:**
- No transfer of Indian user data outside India (Supabase Mumbai region)
- Grievance officer designated (required by IT Rules 2021)
- Privacy policy in Hindi and English

### 31.5 API Security

- Rate limiting: 100 req/min per authenticated user, 20 req/min per IP (unauthenticated)
- Input validation: Pydantic models validate all incoming data
- SQL injection prevention: ORM with parameterized queries; no raw SQL from user input
- XSS prevention: Content Security Policy headers; Sanitize all user-generated content before rendering
- CORS: Strict origin whitelist
